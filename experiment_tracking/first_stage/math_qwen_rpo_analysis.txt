Qwen Math-500 RPO Analysis
==========================

Run identity
------------
CODE: math500_reasoning_qwen_rpo_qwen14opt_lambda1_vs900
Output directory: outputs/math_prompt_optimization/reasoning/rpo/math500_reasoning_qwen_rpo_qwen14opt_lambda1_vs900
Validation: 3 folds x 300 examples = 900 examples
Stable-score penalty (lambda): 1.0
Iterations: 10

Main result
-----------
RPO completed correctly, but it did not find a prompt that outperformed the
initial prompt. All ten rewritten prompts had lower raw accuracy and lower
stable accuracy. RPO therefore correctly retained the initial prompt at both
the fifth- and tenth-iteration snapshots.

Initial/retained prompt:
Solve the following math problem. Think step by step carefully before answering.

The strongest rewritten prompt was iteration 3, but its stable score was still
1.27 percentage points below the initial prompt. Every candidate also had lower
raw accuracy, so changing lambda from 1 to 0 would not have selected any of them.

Prompt analysis
---------------
1. The initial prompt contains 12 words, whereas the rewritten prompts contain
   29-97 words. RPO consistently made the instruction much longer.

2. The new prompts encouraged substantially more reasoning. Average generated
   output length increased from 3,276.85 tokens for the initial prompt to
   3,557.66-4,233.66 tokens for the candidates.

3. Several prompts overfit the small set of feedback examples. They introduced
   narrow instructions concerning rotations, geometric theorems, Jensen's
   inequality, algebraic expansion, and conditional probability rather than a
   broadly useful mathematical strategy.

4. Iterations 4, 5, and 7 added boxed-answer instructions even though answer
   formatting is handled by the separate fixed answer instruction. Explicit
   answer-tag extraction fell from 380 examples initially to only 4 and 2 in
   iterations 5 and 7, respectively. Boxed fallback recovered most answers, but
   the two instructions were still competing with each other.

5. The initial prompt produced 88 examples with no extractable final answer.
   Every candidate was worse on this measure, producing 98-113 unextractable
   answers. The more verbose prompts likely increased overthinking, unfinished
   responses, and failure to reach the required final answer.

Feedback analysis
-----------------
RPO used three feedback examples in every iteration, intentionally consisting
of one correct and two incorrect examples. Across ten iterations, this produced
10 correct and 20 incorrect feedback examples.

However, 17 of the 20 incorrect examples had:

LLM Selected Answer: INVALID

Many of these were final-answer completion or extraction failures rather than
clear mathematical-reasoning errors. In several cases, the reasoning reached or
approached the correct result but did not provide an extractable final answer.
The optimizer sometimes interpreted these cases as correct but mislabeled, or
as external formatting errors. It then generated longer or formatting-oriented
instructions rather than correcting a mathematical reasoning weakness.

Parent selection was not the primary problem. Iterations 1-4 and 9 directly
rewrote the initial prompt, while later iterations sometimes explored children
of weaker candidates. Although this later exploration compounded prompt drift,
the first four direct rewrites of the initial prompt were already worse.

Conclusion
----------
This was not a parser, validation, or RPO-selection failure. All ten rewrite
calls produced valid parsed prompts, and the selection procedure correctly
rejected every weaker candidate. The main problem was that the feedback set was
dominated by INVALID answer/extraction failures and the optimizer converted
those signals into verbose, example-specific instructions.

Proposed next fix
-----------------
For reasoning-prompt optimization, prioritize incorrect training examples that
have a valid extracted final answer. These are more likely to represent genuine
reasoning mistakes, although answer representation can also cause a grading
failure. Handle INVALID and incomplete responses separately: unfinished
reasoning is still a useful optimization signal, but it must not be presented
as a completed mathematical answer that disagrees with the ground truth.

The feedback prompt should also distinguish a genuine mathematical error from
a missing or malformed final answer. Revised instructions should remain concise
and task-general and should not introduce answer-format directions that compete
with the fixed answer instruction.

Follow-up analysis and proposed controlled trial (2026-09-16)
------------------------------------------------------------
This section records the earlier diagnostic proposal. Its recommended trial
is superseded by the user-approved implemented update at the end of this file.

1. Feedback quality, not the stable-score penalty, is the main issue.
   Seventeen of twenty incorrect feedback examples had no extracted final
   answer (85%). Nineteen of twenty incorrect examples also had their reasoning
   compacted to a 2,000-token head/tail excerpt. Compaction may hide an important
   intermediate step; it is not the same thing as generation truncation.

2. Missing final answers are common even for the initial validation prompt.
   Of 110 incorrect validation outcomes, 88 had no extracted final answer and
   22 had an extracted but incorrect final answer. The latter is about 2.44%
   of the 900 validation examples. As a sampling heuristic only, a random
   feedback pool of 100 would yield about 2.4 such errors, whereas 300 would
   yield about 7.3. Training error rates and individual pools may differ.

3. The optimizer sometimes distrusted the supplied outcomes instead of using
   them. Examples include incorrect/INVALID cases whose reasoning reached the
   gold answer but never completed the final response. Feedback described some
   as correct but mislabeled or as external errors. At least one feedback
   explanation also incorrectly challenged the valid half-angle identity
   tan(a/2)tan(b/2) = ((1-cos(a))(1-cos(b)))/(sin(a)sin(b)), when defined.
   Generated feedback is therefore not a reliable mathematical oracle.

4. Longer, topic-specific rewrites encouraged more work, not better completion.
   They added repeated verification or techniques suggested by the few examples.
   All ten rewrites lost both raw and stable accuracy. This is evidence against
   reducing lambda alone as the fix. Longer outputs correlate with poorer
   completion here; this does not establish that length caused every failure.

5. Answer-format constraints were already present in the rewrite meta-prompt.
   It explicitly says not to add answer-format instructions or answer tags.
   Several candidates nevertheless added boxed-answer directions. Do not report
   this as a missing constraint or alter the separate fixed answer instruction.

Recommended first trial, subject to user confirmation:
- Keep lambda = 1, target max_new_tokens = 8192, validation = 3 x 300,
  the existing seed, and the original RPO search procedure.
- Increase the randomized training feedback pool from 100 to about 300.
- Select three incorrect examples preferentially with extracted final answers,
  instead of blindly selecting three INVALID responses. Log shortages and use
  an informative incomplete response as a clearly identified fallback.
- Add one feedback clarification: "If no final answer was extracted,
  distinguish unfinished reasoning from a mathematical error; do not assume
  that the ground-truth answer or outcome is mislabeled."
- Add one rewrite clarification: "Use concise, task-general guidance drawn
  from these examples; avoid topic-specific methods and unnecessary repeated
  verification." Preserve the rest of the original wording and the existing
  no-answer-format constraint.
- If this fails, compare one correct + two incorrect examples using the same
  quality filter. Consider a larger reasoning excerpt or a stronger parent
  preference only as subsequent trials, not simultaneous changes.

Using all three examples as mistakes is reasonable to test, but it is not a
guarantee of improvement. The key change is providing actionable feedback.
Do not relabel unchanged Qwen RPO sources as improvements.

Implemented Math RPO update (2026-09-16; experiments not yet rerun)
----------------------------------------------------------------
User-approved changes supersede the earlier proposal to exclude incomplete
answers or increase the feedback pool to 300:

- Math RPO now selects only incorrect examples, default three, from the existing
  randomized 100-example feedback pool. It uses available errors and logs any
  shortage; it does not fill remaining slots with correct examples. With zero
  errors, no feedback/rewrite generation is performed and snapshots are retained.
- Feedback examples include actual generated output-token count and configured
  output limit. Incorrect outcomes are marked as:
  * reasoning token limit exceeded: no extracted answer and generated count
    reaches the configured limit;
  * missing final answer: no extracted answer without a confirmed limit hit;
  * incorrect final answer: an extracted answer failed the primary grader.
  Correct answers are not assigned a failure category, even at the token limit.
- The Math feedback meta-prompt asks the optimizer to identify repetition,
  unnecessary checking, and detours behind token-limit failures instead of
  treating the provided outcome or ground truth as mislabeled.
- The Math rewrite meta-prompt asks for concise, focused reasoning that reaches
  a final answer within budget, with task-general rather than example-specific
  guidance. The original no-answer-format restriction remains unchanged.
- The separate fixed answer instruction, target limit 8192, optimizer limit
  10000, feedback reasoning excerpt 2000, lambda 1, and search are unchanged.
- Other tasks keep mixed correct/incorrect RPO selection and their original
  feedback/rewrite wording. EvoPrompt, ETGPO, and second-stage flows are unchanged.
- Existing run outputs and historical scores were not changed or rerun. Use a
  new CODE for this trial; do not overwrite the canonical historical RPO run.
