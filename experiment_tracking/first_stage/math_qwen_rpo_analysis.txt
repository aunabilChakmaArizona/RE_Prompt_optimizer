Qwen Math-500 RPO Analysis
==========================

Run identity
------------
CODE: math500_reasoning_qwen_rpo_qwen14opt_lambda1_vs900
Output directory: outputs/math_prompt_optimization/reasoning/rpo/math500_reasoning_qwen_rpo_qwen14opt_lambda1_vs900
Validation: 3 folds x 300 examples = 900 examples
Stable-score penalty (lambda): 1.0
Iterations: 10

Main result
-----------
RPO completed correctly, but it did not find a prompt that outperformed the
initial prompt. All ten rewritten prompts had lower raw accuracy and lower
stable accuracy. RPO therefore correctly retained the initial prompt at both
the fifth- and tenth-iteration snapshots.

Initial/retained prompt:
Solve the following math problem. Think step by step carefully before answering.

| Initial   | 87.78            | 87.36            | Short, general instruction |
| 1         | 86.11            | 85.70            | Multiple approaches and assumptions |
| 2         | 86.00            | 85.02            | Algebra expansion and edge cases |
| 3         | 86.78            | 86.09            | Symmetry, rotations, and overcounting |
| 4         | 86.44            | 86.03            | Exact boxed answer |
| 5         | 85.11            | 83.61            | Multiple methods and boxed answer |
| 6         | 85.56            | 84.06            | Ordering and answer formatting |
| 7         | 86.11            | 84.98            | Geometry and numerical precision |

| 9         | 85.89            | 85.32            | Rationalization and Jensen's inequality |
| 10        | 86.00            | 84.91            | Combinatorics and conditional probability |

The strongest rewritten prompt was iteration 3, but its stable score was still
1.27 percentage points below the initial prompt. Every candidate also had lower
raw accuracy, so changing lambda from 1 to 0 would not have selected any of them.

Prompt analysis
---------------
1. The initial prompt contains 12 words, whereas the rewritten prompts contain
   29-97 words. RPO consistently made the instruction much longer.

2. The new prompts encouraged substantially more reasoning. Average generated
   output length increased from 3,276.85 tokens for the initial prompt to
   3,557.66-4,233.66 tokens for the candidates.

3. Several prompts overfit the small set of feedback examples. They introduced
   narrow instructions concerning rotations, geometric theorems, Jensen's
   inequality, algebraic expansion, and conditional probability rather than a
   broadly useful mathematical strategy.

4. Iterations 4, 5, and 7 added boxed-answer instructions even though answer
   formatting is handled by the separate fixed answer instruction. Explicit
   answer-tag extraction fell from 380 examples initially to only 4 and 2 in
   iterations 5 and 7, respectively. Boxed fallback recovered most answers, but
   the two instructions were still competing with each other.

5. The initial prompt produced 88 examples with no extractable final answer.
   Every candidate was worse on this measure, producing 98-113 unextractable
   answers. The more verbose prompts likely increased overthinking, unfinished
   responses, and failure to reach the required final answer.

Feedback analysis
-----------------
RPO used three feedback examples in every iteration, intentionally consisting
of one correct and two incorrect examples. Across ten iterations, this produced
10 correct and 20 incorrect feedback examples.

However, 17 of the 20 incorrect examples had:

LLM Selected Answer: INVALID

Many of these were final-answer completion or extraction failures rather than
clear mathematical-reasoning errors. In several cases, the reasoning reached or
approached the correct result but did not provide an extractable final answer.
The optimizer sometimes interpreted these cases as correct but mislabeled, or
as external formatting errors. It then generated longer or formatting-oriented
instructions rather than correcting a mathematical reasoning weakness.

Parent selection was not the primary problem. Iterations 1-4 and 9 directly
rewrote the initial prompt, while later iterations sometimes explored children
of weaker candidates. Although this later exploration compounded prompt drift,
the first four direct rewrites of the initial prompt were already worse.

Conclusion
----------
This was not a parser, validation, or RPO-selection failure. All ten rewrite
calls produced valid parsed prompts, and the selection procedure correctly
rejected every weaker candidate. The main problem was that the feedback set was
dominated by INVALID answer/extraction failures and the optimizer converted
those signals into verbose, example-specific instructions.

Proposed next fix
-----------------
For reasoning-prompt optimization, prioritize incorrect training examples that
have a valid extracted final answer. These are more likely to represent genuine
reasoning mistakes. INVALID, truncated, and answer-format-only failures should
be excluded when the editable prompt does not include the fixed answer-format
instruction.

The feedback prompt should also distinguish a genuine mathematical error from
a missing or malformed final answer. Revised instructions should remain concise
and task-general and should not introduce answer-format directions that compete
with the fixed answer instruction.
